Every number as rectangles, primes as lonely crosses

Twelve unit squares can be laid out as a 1×12 strip, a 2×6 rectangle or a 3×4 rectangle. Draw all of a number's rectangles on top of each other, centred, and every number becomes a little star. A prime has only one rectangle, the thin strip 1×p, so its star is a bare cross. Set the stars out on a golden-angle spiral and the pattern, like a Penrose tiling, never repeats.

The star of one number

Put one corner of every rectangle at the origin. The opposite corners all lie on the hyperbola xy = n, because every rectangle has the same area n. Each corner is one way to factor n. A prime has only the two corners far out on the edges, (1, p) and (p, 1). The outline of a star is this staircase under the hyperbola, mirrored into four quadrants, so the more ways a number can be factored, the more its star fills out the smooth hyperbolic shape.

Using all the prime factors as edges gives a box instead of a rectangle, and every number has exactly one such box: 12 is a 2×2×3 box and nothing else. That is the fundamental theorem of arithmetic, as geometry.

Counting all the rectangles

How many rectangles are there with area at most N? Each one is a grid point (a, b) under the hyperbola xy = N, so the count is the number of grid points in that region. Dirichlet showed in 1849 that it follows a smooth curve:

rectangles with area ≤ N = N·ln N + (2γ − 1)·N + Δ(N)

Here γ ≈ 0.5772 is Euler's constant and Δ(N) is a wobble, exactly like the wobble of the circle chain. How big Δ(N) can get is the Dirichlet divisor problem. It is believed to grow no faster than about N1/4, while the best proven bound is about N0.314. Voronoi showed that this wobble too is a sum of waves. It is a sister problem of the Riemann Hypothesis, and it is still open.

Next: grow a tiling from the rectangles whose sides are both prime, 2×2, 2×3, 3×3, 2×5, …, packed as tightly as possible.

Primes are the numbers whose stars are almost empty. Their corners sit alone on the edges of the hyperbola, and how those lonely points are spread out is what the Riemann Hypothesis is about.
